Background

Computer engineering graduate from the University of Toronto (2024). I have diverse software experience in many areas such as full-stack, microservices, web dev, machine learning, and graphics. Currently exploring Rust and agentic AI.

On my own time, I deeply enjoy running, playing the piano, and doing jiu-jitsu.

This portfolio is built with Next.js + MUI — designed to be lightweight, fast, and easy to maintain. Cline + OpenRouter LLMs were used to help with this current overhaul.

Why do I love engineering?

Bold of me to say "engineering" in general, eh? Especially considering the fact that almost all of my experience and projects on this website are exclusively software. Truth be told, however, I've messed with electronics, mechanical systems, and, naturally, robotics in the past. As diverse as these areas are, I can't help but think there's something common among all of these.

Technicalities?

I was actually discussing this with a good friend of mine - he asked me what do I truly like about coding. With any form of engineering, there's the obvious forms of satisfaction: with software, I can leverage my skill in algorithms and mathematics and make some seriously good predictions about what my design will do, or how adjustments to a piece of software will change the outcome. On the other hand, in an area like mechanical systems, I can leverage my skill in dynamics and make some good predictions about how the system will react or change depending on what I do with it. We can see there's a trend emerging here and its often one of the first things people think of with engineering: using a bunch of smart, "technical" things and applying it to a problem.

This certainly resonates with me on some level as I do like using skills that I've honed over the years. But I do feel like this reasoning is empty and outright based on ego if this reasoning exists in isolation.

There's more to it

Let's bring in something I love doing that isn't STEM: art and music. While I've allowed my visual art prowess to decline over the years, I managed to teach myself piano and express myself through that. I can create something new when I play the piano, something that isn't fully explainable with deterministic or rationalist laws. If we compare this to engineering, we can see in both cases, we're creating something new with different mediums. With art, the medium is a lot more free with far less constraints. There's less of a purpose and there's no real need for one. With engineering, there are certainly more constraints and more criteria to guide whatever you're making, and definitely a lot more consequence.

But at the end of the day, there's something deeply satisfying and moving about creating something new, regardless of whatever the medium is. Even if many choices can be explained rationally, there's definitely something that cannot be explained so easily here.
